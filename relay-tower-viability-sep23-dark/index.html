This week’s takeaways

  1. KPI retention did not move, same as Barracks

    Relay D1 is 21.8% vs Control 20.2% (103/473 vs 88/436). That is a small edge, not a meaningful KPI change. D2–D5, starts, and minutes are also a wash. Both arms sit around the 21% D1 mark this week — the same retention outcome we saw with Free Barracks: in-match play can change without day-1 return changing. D7 is not mature.

  2. Match 5 is the one established play win

    First-attempt win rate is 54.2% vs 37.6%. Reach to Match 5 is the same (~26% of the cohort). Completed matches run about nine seconds shorter. Player-average win fraction is also higher (0.867 vs 0.816).

  3. Players buy cheaper land, not more of it

    Cheap barracks +35%, question tiles +14%, gold mines −22%, medium barracks −70%. The same mix shows up at a fixed Match 3 and on build 2.11.2. Chests, store, battle pass, and cards did not move.

  4. Towers get used. Match 1 does not teach the pair.

    87% of complete matches capture a tower; 59% activate a pair. Only 21% of Match 1 pairs, covering 1.9% of that match. Once two towers are owned, 95% pair. The missing step is claiming the second tower, not drawing the line.

  5. The connecting line is not doing most of the work

    Among buffed hero spawns, 77% are local adjacency only; line-only is 7%. 80% of first pairs stay on the home side. Match 5 wins more often with a pair (68% vs 41%), but players are already about +38 tiles ahead — do not read that as a causal line effect.

  6. Tutorial looks slower. Match 1 reach is not worse.

    Tutorial passage is 67% vs 79%, and completers take 66s vs 31s. Formal Match 1 reach is 54% vs 50%. Do not call tutorial completion overall onboarding loss. Iterate Match 5 and first-match second-tower teaching; slot capacity is the friction (2,441 no-slot rejections).

Coming back is a wash — including the middle of the distribution

After Holm correction on the nine primary tests, D1–D5, Match-1 reach, starts, minutes, and completion are not different. That is not proof they are equal: the D1 gap still spans about −3.7 to +6.9 percentage points. The minute wash is not one whale hiding a Relay advantage. Players capture towers. They rarely pair in Match 1.

Capture is common. Connecting is not the first-match lesson. Once two towers are owned, pairing is almost automatic (95%). The gap is claiming the second tower: 87% of matches unlock two ownership slots, but only 62% ever own two towers. Pairing usually fires at the same second the second tower is captured (median delay 0s, median elapsed 103s).

The buff is local. Friction is slots, not a tug-of-war.

86% of successful captures are on the home side. When several legal towers exist, players pick a nearest one 88% of the time. Among buffed hero spawns, 77% are local adjacency only; line-only is 7%.

67% of closed legal capture opportunities end in a capture; 13% close because capacity is full. Median time from becoming legal to a successful capture is 1.2 seconds among takes. Contest is rare: 244 pair route-cuts, 20% recovered. Players recapture a previously owned tower twice in the whole sample.

Complete summaries also show 36% of barracks-time supplied and 34% of hero spawns buffed. Equal-weighting players drops supplied barracks-time to 28%, so longer sessions get more exposure.

Do not read Match 5 pair wins as a causal tower effect Within Relay, Match 5 first attempts win 68.1% (47/69) with a pair vs 41.3% (31/75) without (within-Relay FDR q=0.012). Players are already about +38 tiles ahead when the first pair fires. Reaching Match 6 that day is 89.9% vs 86.7% — not a continuation jump. Other match indices do not survive that correction.
