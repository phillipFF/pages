The game

Warcraft III

Reign of Chaos released July 3, 2002 in the United States and Australia, and July 5 in Europe. The Frozen Throne expansion released July 1, 2003.

1 million Sold worldwide in the first month. The fastest-selling PC game at the time, ahead of Diablo II. Third-best-selling computer game of 2002.
4.4 million Copies shipped to stores for the launch. A shipment, not a sales total.
Over 3 million Reign of Chaos sold by May 2003, when the expansion went gold.
92 Metacritic. GameSpot's game of the year. The Interactive Achievement Award for computer strategy.

The expansion

The Frozen Throne had initial orders over 2.5 million. It sold more than 1 million copies by August 15, 2003.

Who played

Matches run on Battle.net, with anonymous ladder matchmaking. Blizzard did not publish a lifetime player or install total.

Why it lasted

The World Editor shipped with the game. Defense of the Ancients, a player-made map, is where the MOBA genre comes from.

Reforged, January 28, 2020. Both games in one client. Metacritic scored it 59. The update replaced the classic client, custom games and ranked play were worse at launch, and Blizzard offered refunds.

The match

Core Gameplay Loop

  1. 01Gather two scarce resources. Workers are split between them, and a mine runs out.
  2. 02Balance resource spend between buildings, units, and tech. Gold in a building or an upgrade is gold that is not in the army.
  3. 03Take your hero and army to explore the map. Clear camps, enemy bases, and expand to new territories. Increase your resource generation through new bases and camps.
  4. 04Multi-task across crucial decision points by balancing your time across commanding your army, resource production, upgrading your base tech ladders, and training more units. Crush the enemy bases to win.

Against a traditional RTS

How It Differentiates from a Traditional RTS

Warcraft III keeps this package. These are the places it behaves differently.

Traditional RTS

Core Traits

  • Real-Time Gameplay
  • Resource Gathering
  • Base Building
  • Unit Production
  • Tech Upgrades
  • Single session battle
  • Command of multiple units & groups
  • Top down overhead or isometric camera control
  • Simultaneous Macro vs. Micro gameplay
Warcraft III

Differentiations

  • Choose a Hero to enhance your army and cast battle ending spells
  • Utilize items and equipment to customize your strategy
  • Night time changes rules of the game
  • Create new paths in the map through resource collection
  • Creeping: Destroy neutral camps for items and hero XP
  • Less is more: smaller army sizes, tankier HP pools, slower fights for more focused battles
  • Food Limits: Reduced resource output for exceeding unit limitations
06 / 20

Takeaway

Player Feedback

  1. 01Players treat the hero as the center of the match. A level and an item count as their own advantage, beside gold and the map.
  2. 02Creeps are the piece players say other RTS games do not have. Clearing a camp well takes routes and tricks that are not written on the unit.
  3. 03Upkeep is the other system players argue over. Staying under the food line keeps full gold income, so a player can sit at home and bank it. Blizzard added the tax to punish that. In some matchups, the player who has to fight on the map is the one who pays.
  4. 04Fights stay small and slow. Players stand their units around an enemy hero so it cannot walk out, and that trap can decide the fight. That same clumsy movement is what players from other RTS games reject. A few heroes are strong from the altar, and the rest of the roster barely shows up.

Scarcity

Scarcity in Multiple Resources

Workers gather gold or lumber. A gold mine depletes, so the first base is a timer. A worker sent to one resource is absent from the other.

01

Depleting resources

The next mine is guarded. Taking it means clearing a camp, and waiting too long starves the base.

02

Different types of resources

Gold feeds the army. Lumber feeds the buildings. Both are required, and they are not substitutes.

03

Which one you need more

The split moves. Early lumber for the chain, then gold for the army, then lumber again for the next tier.

Five workers is the efficient number on a mine when the hall sits beside it.

08 / 20

Food

Unit Production Limits

Every unit costs food. The army caps at 100.

01

The price

A worker, a soldier, and a hero each spend food. The number is on the unit.

02

The stop

Production ends at 100 food. Buildings supply food up to that line, and they do not move it.

03

Not the tax

Crossing a food line can also cut gold income. That is a separate system. The cap is the hard stop.

Upkeep

Income Tax on Army Size

Past a food line, gold income falls. Lumber comes in full no matter how large the army is. The limited mine makes the tax a reason to move.

50 food and under

No upkeep

Gold income stays whole.

51 to 80 food

Low upkeep

Gold income drops to 70%.

81 food and above

High upkeep

Gold income drops to 40%.

These bands are The Frozen Throne's, and Reforged kept them.

Build gates

Prerequisite Build Gates

Multiple building ladders that unlock new tiers and units.

Warcraft III

The chain is the lock

Tier 1

Hall, then a production building, then the unit. No building, no unit.

Tier 2 and 3

Upgrade the hall. The next buildings appear, and their units with them.

Upgrades

A separate building. It sells attack, armor, or a new behavior.

Human shape of the same lock: Town Hall, Barracks, Footman. Keep is the second tier. The Blacksmith sells the upgrades. The other races use different buildings and the same gates.

Heroes

Persistent Hero Growth

Experience, skill points, auras, and customizable item builds. The piece that enhances your strategy. You pick the hero. Each level gives a skill point. Most heroes can fill every skill by level 10.

01

How you enhance your units

Auras and buffs ride with the hero. The army changes because of where the hero stands and which skill you took.

02

XP and leveling

Camps and fights pay experience inside the match. A level is the result of what you just did.

03

Unique skills and passives

Some skills are casts. Some are always on. Two heroes with the same army are different plans.

Strength, agility, and intelligence rise on their own. The player does not spend points on them. Ultimate skills open at level 6.

Fog

Fog of War

Unseen ground stays black. An army outside your sight is hidden.

01

Never seen

The map starts black. Camps, mines, and the enemy base stay unknown until a unit walks there.

02

Seen, then left

Explored ground keeps its shape. Units on it disappear when nothing of yours can see them.

03

Sight is local

A unit or a building sees a radius around itself. Allies share that sight.

Night shortens that radius for most races. That clock is a separate system.

The pay

Gold, a level, an item

Each creep pays some gold. The hero gains experience. The strongest creep in the camp is usually the one that drops an item.

The cost

The army is the price

Units walk out, take damage, and sometimes die. That time is not spent on the opponent's base.

What stays

What stays on the hero

Gold is a bounty. The level and the item stay. A cleared camp does not come back, so the walk is a race to be first.

Creep experience stops at hero level 5. After that, a camp still pays gold and items. Further levels come from fighting players.

Damage types

Damage Type Matchups

Different attack types balancing each other in a rock, paper, scissors meta.

Attack is Normal, Pierce, Siege, Magic, Chaos, or Hero. Armor is Light, Medium, Heavy, Fortified, Unarmored, or Hero. The percent is not printed on the unit.

Upgrades

Upgrades That Change the Job

Attack and armor for a class of unit, or a new behavior on one.

A class

Attack and armor

Bought at a building. Applied to a group, such as melee or ranged. The units hit harder or take less. The job stays.

A unit

A new behavior

A Footman learns Defend. A Headhunter becomes a Berserker. An Archer mounts a Hippogryph. The unit is for something new.

16 / 20

Day and night

Rules That Change with Time

At night, sight falls and most creeps sleep. One race can hide.

Both players

A clock on the match

The map cycles between day and night. At night, sight drops, and nearly all creeps sleep. A sleeping camp does not chase you. Hit one, and the camp wakes.

One race

The exception

Night Elves keep their sight. A Night Elf unit that stands still can hide. The sleep is the rule both players feel. The hide is the rule on one race.

Multi-tasking

Multi-tasking Gameplay

Balancing act between commanding units, map control, building expansion, and unit production.

At the base

The queue keeps going

Unit production, the worker split, and the next expansion. It runs while you look at the field, and it stops when the queue is empty.

On the map

The army does not wait

Commanding units, the camp, and the path. It goes wrong in the time it takes to place a building.

The balancing act. Watch the fight too long and the base goes idle. Watch the base too long and a unit dies on the map.

A new RTS

Interesting Differentiation Mechanics

  1. 1aIterate on different ways to represent and interact with the Hero of the map. Many versions and methods here.
  2. 1bItems on Heroes. Worth holding outside the match as a larger meta layer, so the match itself stays simple.
  3. 03Day and night is not a game changer. It is interesting to brainstorm on top of.
  4. 04Opening new paths is not a big differentiator either. It needs more thought before it is worth pursuing.
  5. 05Creeping is an interesting addition. Would not make much impact in its original implementation, would need exploration around making it the main objective (PvPvE / Indirect PvP).
  6. 06A smaller map, a smaller army, and more focused fights. Worth exploring how much each unit weighs, and an easier time showcasing the character's animation and art.
  7. 07A food cap is an interesting layer to add, but it is not impactful enough on its own. It only shows up late, and it needs a partner such as merging.
  8. 08Damage types as rock-paper-scissors countering. Different ways to implement this, in unit type, armor type, and so on.

Hero RPG

Takeaway

Lots of mechanics explored already in the Hero RPG prototype today, but only as one version. Worth revisiting and trying different versions of the concept.

Simplification is often better for mobile. Lower the number of decisions and actions.

Playable Ideas - Iterate on the Hero Mechanic

Movement

  • Stick control. The hero walks, troops follow.
  • Tap a point. The hero paths there, troops follow.
  • Steer the hero. The troops stay with the hero.

Skill usage

  • Skills on a cooldown
  • Skills on resource usage (mana)
  • Skills auto fire when a target is in range.
  • Skills are manually triggered.
  • Skills require gestures like flicks and swipes.

Hero selection

  • Chosen before the match and locked.
  • Two heroes at the start. Commit, then the fight starts.
  • Spawn one of three heroes of your choice every minute.

Leveling cadence

  • XP on a timer, so every fight hits the same beats.
  • XP on a timer, so a won exchange is the XP.
  • Choose your skills vs. auto selection

Item drops

  • The gear is equipped outside the match.
  • Items can be picked up during the match.

When the hero falls

  • Match ends if your hero dies
  • Heroes can be respawned on a timer
  • Heroes can be purchased again with resources after dying
